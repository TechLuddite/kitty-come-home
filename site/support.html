Support & credits

How to help, what money would actually buy, and who is owed credit for the work this is built on.

If your cat is missing right now, close this page. None of it will help you tonight. Go back to the protocol and come back in a month when she is home. This page is deliberately kept off the search path, and there is no donation prompt anywhere in the protocol itself.

Tell someone about it

The best protocol nobody finds is worth nothing. If you know somebody who works at a shelter or a rescue, send them the link. If you are in a local lost-pet group, put it there. The people who need this have never heard of it and will not go looking until the worst night of their year.

Free · helps most

Print it and hand it out

The protocol prints as a handout on purpose, and it is CC BY 4.0 so you may copy, translate, alter and distribute it, including commercially. Pin it to a noticeboard. Leave a stack at a vet's desk. No permission needed and no credit required beyond attribution.

Correct it if it is wrong

This is the most valuable thing anyone can give this project. If you do this work, shelter or rescue staff, TNR trappers, animal control, trained searchers, or someone who found their own cat and learned something the hard way, and something here is wrong or missing, please say so. Field experience counts as evidence and you do not need a citation. This is the part where being wrong has a real cost.

Give to a shelter instead

Said plainly, because it is true: your money does more good at your local shelter or rescue than it does here. They are the ones running the intake desk, holding the strays, lending out the humane traps and paying the vet bills. This project is a web page and a set of instructions, and it costs almost nothing to run.

If you came here intending to give something because a page like this helped you, give it to them. That is the more sensible option and there is no catch in saying so.

The Missing Animal Response Network is also worth your attention. Much of what is on the protocol page comes from their work, and they train the people who go out and find animals for a living.

If you still want to support this project

One donation path, and here is exactly what it pays for, so you can check later whether it happened.

What the money buys. Two or three cheap trail cameras and the memory cards for them, to build a labelled set of real night infrared footage. The project wants to ship a feature that sifts a night's several hundred motion clips down to the few containing a cat, and that cannot be built or honestly evaluated without real footage from a real feeding station. Academic benchmarks on clean daylight photographs are not evidence for it. After that, printing costs for protocol handouts to give to shelters.

That is the whole list. Hosting is free, the database tier is free, and there are no salaries. If the list ever gets longer, it will be written here.

Thanks

Kept as its own section rather than folded into the donation copy, because it is credit rather than sponsorship.

Huang, Coradini, Rand, Morton, Albrecht, Wasson and Robertson

Their 2018 study of 1,210 missing cats in Animals is the evidence base for almost every number on this site, and it is open access. Without it this would be one more page of confident advice with nothing behind it. The distinction between what their data supports and what it merely suggests is the reason the protocol can be honest about its own weak points.

doi.org/10.3390/ani8010005

Kat Albrecht and the Missing Animal Response Network

The behavioural model this whole protocol rests on, that a displaced indoor cat hides close by rather than roaming, and will often not answer when called, comes from their field practice, published freely for anyone to use. They have been saying it for two decades against the grain of standard lost-pet advice.

missinganimalresponse.com

Petco Love Lost, and PawBoost

Credited here because the protocol sends people to them rather than competing with them. Petco Love Lost runs a free national lost-and-found database with photo matching, fed by roughly 3,000 shelters. Building a worse copy of it would have been the obvious move and the wrong one.

lost.petcolove.org

Shelter, rescue and TNR volunteers

Nobody named, because the list would be wrong. The humane traps get lent out by people doing this unpaid at 2am, and the protocol assumes throughout that they will help, because they reliably do.

Software

This site has no dependencies. It is hand-written HTML, CSS and JavaScript with no framework, no build step and no package manager, so there is no dependency tree to credit and nothing that can rot underneath it. That is a deliberate choice for a page someone may need to load on bad signal in a dark garden.

Hosted on GitHub Pages, at no cost. The database groundwork for the planned search tool runs on Supabase's free tier.
