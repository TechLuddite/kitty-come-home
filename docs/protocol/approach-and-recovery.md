# When you find it

You have eyeshine under a deck at 2am, or a shape at the back of a shed. **What you do in the
next sixty seconds decides whether this ends tonight or continues for another week.**

## Do not rush it

The instinct — relief, then lunging forward, then a raised happy voice — is exactly wrong. A
displaced cat in survival mode reads a fast approach as a predator. It will bolt, and it will
relocate somewhere you have not searched, often further away and more frightened than before.

**Assume it will not recognise you.** Behave as though you are approaching an animal that has
never met you.

## The approach

1. **Stop moving the moment you see it.** Do not take another step.
2. **Get low.** Sit or crouch. Standing over a hiding cat is threatening.
3. **Look away, or half away.** A direct stare is a threat signal. Blink slowly.
4. **Speak quietly**, in the normal soft voice you use at home. Do not raise your voice in relief.
5. **Wait.** Minutes, not seconds. Let it decide you are safe.
6. **Do not shine the torch directly at its face.** Angle the beam to the ground nearby so you can
   still see it.
7. If it is in a confined space with one exit, **do not block the exit**. A cornered cat panics.

## If it comes to you

Let it come the whole way. Do not reach out. When it is against you and settled, pick it up
calmly and firmly, hold it against your body, and go straight inside without stopping to celebrate.

## If it will not come

This is common and it is not a bad sign. Do not force it.

- **Leave food** right there and back off out of sight.
- **Note the exact location** and treat it as a confirmed sighting.
- **Move the feeding station and camera to that spot** tonight.
- If you can get it, **set a humane trap** there — see [`humane-trapping.md`](humane-trapping.md).
  For a cat that is visible but unapproachable, the trap is usually the answer, and pushing the
  approach instead is usually what loses it.
- Re-search that spot at the same hour tomorrow. Cats keep a schedule.

## If it is somewhere you cannot reach

Under a deck, up a tree, inside a wall cavity, under a building.

- **Do not try to force it out.** No hose, no broom, no smoke, no dog.
- Leave food at the opening and set up the camera to confirm it is still there.
- For a genuinely inaccessible space, ask the property owner about access panels, and ask your
  local shelter or a MAR-trained searcher — they have equipment and have done it before.
- Cats come down from trees on their own, usually at night, usually when nobody is watching.
  Leave food at the base and give it the night before escalating.

## If it is injured

Get it contained and to a vet immediately. A frightened, injured cat will bite and scratch hard
regardless of temperament — use a towel or a blanket and a carrier, protect your hands and arms,
and do not try to assess the injury in the field.

## Afterwards

- Keep it inside, in one quiet room, for at least a day.
- Expect it to hide and to be unlike itself for several days. That is normal.
- Vet check within a day or two even if it looks fine.
- Take the posters down, update your listings, and thank the neighbours. They will ask.
