# Feeding station and camera

This is what searches while you sleep, and over a long search it is where most confirmed
sightings come from. Set it up on night one.

## Where

**At the point of escape**, the actual door or window. Not the front porch because it is
convenient. The cat is navigating by familiar smell, and that is the spot with the most of it.

If you have a credible sighting elsewhere, add a second station there. Keep the first one.

## What to put out

1. **Strong-smelling food.** Tinned tuna, sardines, or the smelliest wet food you have. Smell is
   the whole point, dry food does not carry. Refresh it daily; it goes off and stops working.
2. **Water.**
3. **Your cat's litter tray, unemptied.** Counter-intuitive and genuinely useful, it is the
   strongest available scent marker of home territory.
4. **Something that smells of you and of home**, an unwashed t-shirt, its bed, a blanket it slept
   on.
5. **A shelter**, if the weather is poor: a box or carrier on its side, opening away from the wind,
   with the bedding inside. Cats will use a covered station when they will not approach an open one.

**Do not put out anything that draws a crowd of other animals if you can avoid it.** You will get
raccoons, opossums, skunks and other cats regardless, that is expected, and it is exactly the
problem the camera triage is meant to solve. But food left in a wide-open bowl in the middle of a
lawn maximises it.

## The camera

Anything that records motion at night is worth having. In rough order of usefulness:

1. **A trail camera / game camera.** Best option by a distance. Cheap, infrared, battery-powered,
   weatherproof, records to an SD card, and designed for exactly this. Available for very little
   second-hand.
2. **An existing doorbell or security camera** aimed at the station.
3. **An old phone** on a charger, propped in a window, running a motion-detection app. Poor at
   night without light, but better than nothing.

Setup notes:

- Mount it **low**, roughly 30 to 45 cm off the ground. Cameras set at human height look straight
  over a cat.
- Aim it **at the food**, filling the frame with the station rather than the whole garden. Fewer
  false triggers, better identifying detail.
- Point it **away from the road** and away from neighbouring windows and doors. This matters
  legally and neighbourly, and it also cuts the false-trigger rate enormously.
- Check that infrared night mode is on, and that the date and time are set correctly. **The
  timestamp is data**, it tells you the hour the cat is moving, which tells you when to search.

## Reading the results

Expect **hundreds of clips a night**, nearly all of them useless: wind in a shrub, a moth in the
IR beam, raccoons, a neighbour's cat, a possum.

Reviewing it manually before work is an hour you do not have. This is the specific problem the
project's camera triage feature exists to solve, see [`../ai-scope.md`](../ai-scope.md), and it
is not built yet. For now:

- **Sort by time and skim the 11pm to 5am window first.** That is when cats move.
- Look for the **eyeshine and the low, smooth movement**. Raccoons and possums move very
  differently from cats, and after one night you will spot the difference instantly.
- **Keep everything for a week.** A clip that means nothing tonight means a lot after a sighting
  three streets over.

## Confirming it is your cat

A cat at the station is not necessarily your cat. Neighbourhood cats find free tuna very quickly.

Look for markings rather than overall impression, tail tip, chest patch, the exact stripe pattern
on the flank, one ear notch. Night IR footage is greyscale, so **colour tells you nothing.**
Pattern and proportion are all you have.

**Do not conclude on one bad clip either way.** The most common mistake is deciding a blurred grey
shape is definitely not your cat, taking the station down, and losing the one thing that was
working.

## When you confirm your cat is visiting

Now you have a location and a time window, and the job changes from searching to recovery.

- **Do not chase it.** A displaced cat that gets frightened at the station may not come back.
- Keep the station in exactly the same place and keep it stocked.
- Search the immediate area hard during the day, looking for where it is hiding between visits.
  It will be close.
- Consider a humane trap. See [`humane-trapping.md`](humane-trapping.md).
